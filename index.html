Why a nonprofit specialist

Nonprofit books aren't small-business books.

A generalist bookkeeper can categorize your expenses. But nonprofits answer to boards, funders, and auditors — and each expects your numbers in a specific shape.

Restricted funds have to stay restricted in your books, not just in your head. When a funder gives you $50,000 for one program, your accounting has to prove that's where it went.

Grant reports have deadlines. Tracked correctly in QuickBooks, they take minutes.

Audits reward preparation. Clean, consistent, well-documented books make audits shorter, cheaper, and far less stressful — for you and your staff.

You should be able to answer these in five minutes:

  • How much of our cash is actually unrestricted?
  • How much is left to spend on each active grant?
  • What did Program A truly cost last quarter?
  • Could we hand our auditor clean books tomorrow?

If any of these takes longer than five minutes, we should talk.
